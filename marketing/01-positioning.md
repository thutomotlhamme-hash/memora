# Memora: positioning and messaging

## The one-liner

**Memora is the one link for a funeral: their story, every stop with directions, the programme and a QR code, made on your phone in an evening.**

## Who we serve

| Segment | Who they are | What they need that day | Best angle |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **The organiser** (primary) | The child, sibling or cousin who ends up "doing the programme". Usually 25–45, on WhatsApp all day, short on time. | Get the programme done, tell 300 people where to be, stop answering "where is the church?" | *"One link answers everyone."* |
| **The giver** | Friends, colleagues, a church group, or family abroad who can't be there. | A meaningful, practical way to help that isn't another casserole or cash in an envelope. | *"Take one thing off their plate."* (Gift) |
| **The diaspora** | South Africans in the UK, Australia, the Gulf and elsewhere, who miss funerals at home. | To be part of it: see the programme, follow the day, share the memorial. | *"Be there, even from far."* (Gift + Live Funeral Mode) |
| **The multipliers** | Church secretaries, printers and copy shops, burial society and stokvel committees, community WhatsApp admins. | Something that makes their job easier and makes them look good. | *"Your members get it done tonight."* |

## Positioning statement

For South African families organising a funeral, Memora is the memorial and funeral-day guide that replaces the scramble of programme files, location pins and repeated WhatsApp questions with one beautiful link. Unlike printed programmes alone or free memorial sites, Memora maps every stop with exact pins, becomes a live "where to be now" guide on the day, and produces the printable programme and keepsakes from the same details.

## The promise (in order)

1. **Less stress:** one place, one link, one QR, and no retyping for the printer.
2. **Nobody gets lost:** exact pins at the right gate, with one-tap Google Maps, Apple Maps or Waze.
3. **Beautiful and dignified:** a memorial the family is proud to share.
4. **It lasts:** public for a year (through the unveiling), with keepsakes to download.

## Price framing

- **Anchor:** 100 printed A4 programmes cost about R1 000. A newspaper death notice costs R500–R2 000. Memora is **R899 once-off**, and families still print; we give them the print-ready PDF.
- **Free to try:** "Build and preview everything free. Pay only when you publish."
- **Never** discount in a way that feels like a sale on grief ("Black Friday funerals"). Use partner codes and gifts instead.

## Tone of voice

- Calm, warm, plain. Short sentences. Like a kind cousin who knows how to get things done.
- Use the loved one's name, not "the deceased".
- Say **funeral, memorial, programme, the day, the family**. Avoid "product", "solution", "platform", "leverage".
- Respect faith and tradition without assuming one: *service*, *prayer*, *hymns*, *unveiling*, *after-tears*.
- South African English spelling: *programme*, *organise*, *colour*.

## Ethics: how we market around death

1. **Never cold-message the bereaved.** No buying lists, no scraping death notices, no DMs to people who just posted about a loss. We market to the *living network* (organisers, givers, churches), and we let every shared memorial do the talking.
2. **Consent first (POPIA).** Only message people who contacted us, redeemed a gift, or opted in. Gift recipients are contacted only about their gift.
3. **No fear, no guilt.** Never "Don't let your mother's funeral be a mess."
4. **No sad stock photos of coffins.** Use flowers, candles, hands, light, and real (permissioned) memorials.
5. **Always easy to say no.** Every broadcast has "Reply STOP and we won't message you again."
